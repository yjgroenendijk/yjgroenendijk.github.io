    Research

    Social relationships, institutions and change

    My research asks how people create belonging, sustain social connections and participate in everyday life under changing social, technological and institutional conditions. It began with social justice and educational policy, moved to ageing, migration and digital place-making, and now extends to care environments, organisational practices and accessibility.

    Theoretical orientation

    I take a relational and context-sensitive approach to agency: people's opportunities for action are shaped through everyday practices, social ties and institutional arrangements. The strands below connect my published research without implying that every study uses the same theoretical framework.

    Place-making and belonging

    Drawing on digital place-making, mediated mobility and relational understandings of place, my studies examine how older migrants establish social connections and a sense of home across locations. JEMS  ·  MMC

    Participation and peer expertise

    My Warm Experts 3.0 concept examines older adults as producers of technical knowledge, digital care and relational support, rather than only recipients of assistance. It draws on peer production and digital and relational labour. IJCS

    Recognition, justice and institutions

    My earlier work explored context-sensitive understandings of social justice and interculturality. My co-authored Houniao Frame account examines how the Hukou system shapes contrasting media representations of older migrants, linking recognition to institutional constraints. Media Asia

    Organisational behaviour

    In collaborative research, I have examined mechanisms connecting AI adoption, employee experiences, social exchanges and workplace conditions. AI adoption  ·  Customer empowerment

    Research cases

    Migratory Birds in the Digital Age · How do people make a home across places?

    For my PhD, I studied older Chinese seasonal migrants, known as Houniao, through interviews, observation and digital ethnography. My research traced their everyday activities across two homes and how they narrated mobility online. An analysis of 121 Douyin vlogs showed older creators sharing local knowledge, building audience relationships and presenting later life as a period of continued exploration.

    Vlogging study  ·  PhD dissertation

    Ageing in place · What makes a neighbourhood accessible in everyday life?

    At TU/e, I contribute to ELLI and MOVUS, which examine ageing and care in homes and neighbourhoods. In ELLI, Living Labs in Waalre and Emmen involve residents and local partners. My work translates accessibility, agency, participation and person–environment fit into research questions, observations and field instruments. I also study how technologies fit care routines and what shapes their use, adaptation or non-use.

    ELLI project

    Memory as Care · Who gets to tell Rotterdam's story?

    As a co-applicant to this funded participatory oral-history project, I contributed to the proposal and research design with Erasmus University Rotterdam, Erasmus MC, TU/e and community partners. The planned work includes community memory cafés, voluntary interviews supported by photographs and a public exhibition, with separate choices about participation, consent and public sharing.

    Project announcement

    Emerging research agenda · Collective capabilities and shared responsibility

    My next questions concern how everyday knowledge and informal support develop into sustained capacities for joint action, and how responsibility for care is distributed across residents, families, organisations and public institutions. I am particularly interested in technology-mediated care, long-term care and dementia-friendly neighbourhoods. This is a developing agenda, distinct from the findings of my published work.

    How I work

    Mediated nearby

    I use the mediated nearby as a sensitising idea for exploring how important people, places and routines become reachable through everyday and digital practices.

    Choosing methods for the question

    I draw on interviews, participant observation, digital ethnography, narrative inquiry and participatory oral history, with narrative, thematic or framing analysis according to the research question and material.

    Research ethics in practice

    I have prepared research ethics applications and data management plans. In participatory oral history, I pay particular attention to the distinction between consent to an interview and permission to share names, quotations or photographs publicly.
