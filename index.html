    Yongjian Groenendijk-Li, PhD

    Interdisciplinary social scientist

    How do people build belonging, sustain social connections, and participate in a changing society?

    I am an interdisciplinary social scientist working across ageing, migration, care and digital life. My research examines how people navigate social relationships and institutions, create a sense of belonging, and maintain agency and participation amid social and technological change.

    Drawing on ethnography, narrative inquiry and participatory methods, I connect everyday experiences with wider social and institutional conditions. My current work extends these interests to ageing-friendly environments, technology-mediated care and the collective capabilities needed to support inclusive communities.

    My academic background spans public administration and public policy, media and communication, and built environment research. I am currently a postdoctoral researcher at Eindhoven University of Technology and previously taught at Erasmus University Rotterdam.

    Theoretical orientation

    Across my research, I understand agency as situated and relational: people develop ways to act, belong and participate through everyday practices and social connections, while institutions, technologies and inequalities shape their opportunities. My published work draws on digital place-making, mediated mobility, relational and digital care labour, critical framing, and scholarship on social justice and institutional practices.

    My conceptual contributions include Warm Experts 3.0, on older adults’ peer knowledge and care work, and the Houniao Frame, on how institutional constraints shape representations of ageing migrants. I am extending these interests towards collective capabilities and shared responsibility in care. Explore my theoretical approach.

    Selected research

    Migration, digital place-making and belonging

    My doctoral research followed older Chinese seasonal migrants and examined how they use digital media to organise everyday life, maintain social connections and make a sense of home across places.

    Ageing, care and accessible neighbourhoods

    At TU/e, I work across ageing and care research, including ELLI and MOVUS. My work addresses accessibility, participation and agency in residential care and neighbourhood settings, including how technologies fit everyday practices.

    Memory as Care

    In Rotterdam, I contribute to a funded participatory oral-history project with older Chinese residents. It explores how people can share memories on their own terms and how their experiences become part of the city’s public memory.

    Teaching and academic record

    I taught BA, pre-master’s and MA students at Erasmus University Rotterdam in research methods, academic skills and communication, supervised more than 20 internships and received the 2025 PCMC Teaching Award.
